Reviewer guidance

Review evidence safely

Guidance for clinicians, agencies, employers, courts, and platforms reviewing unfamiliar mobility aids and source-linked evidence.

Reviewer roles

Different reviewers ask different questions, but each should preserve the distinction between functional evidence, accommodation analysis, safety analysis, and legal conclusions.

Clinicians

Review functional limitation, endurance, pain, fall risk, gait impact, and whether the evidence is consistent with the reported mobility need.

Agencies

Assess disability-related use, physical accommodation, environment-specific restrictions, and direct threat using actual facts.

Employers

Evaluate worksite access, essential functions, reasonable accommodation options, and avoidable disability burden.

Courts

Separate record evidence from argument, preserve source boundaries, and avoid categorical rules unsupported by the setting.

Platforms

Label records, transformations, surrogate labels, duplicates, and confidence limits so downstream review remains auditable.

Public accommodations

Use individualized review before denying access based on unfamiliar appearance or recreation assumptions.

Safe review practices

Do

Ask what the device does, what environment is at issue, what facts support any safety concern, and what narrower accommodation remains possible.

Do not

Convert wearable records into medical, legal, or access conclusions without preserving uncertainty, caveats, and the reviewer’s role.
