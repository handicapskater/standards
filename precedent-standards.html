Precedent Standards for Non-Standard Mobility Aid Review

Converting the HandicapSkater record into general standards for future review.

Purpose

The HandicapSkater record can be translated into general review standards for non-standard mobility aids. These standards focus on function, disability related use, biomechanics where available, physical accommodation, actual safety risk, effective alternatives, transportation access, and source linked evidence boundaries.

This page does not ask reviewers to copy a prior outcome. It asks reviewers to preserve function, context, source authority, physical accommodation, actual safety risk, effective alternatives, and evidence boundaries.

The standards forming records are summarized on HandicapSkater.com, including the 2001 work access / tax recognition record, the 2007 DOT / FTA transit review, and the 2022 DMV vehicle operation recognition record. The individual documents and case materials live on HandicapSkater.com.

From Record to Standard

The HandicapSkater record is used here as a standards-forming case study. The standards are not limited to inline skates. They are intended to guide review of non-standard mobility aids by function, disability-related use, actual risk, effective alternatives, movement burden, and source-linked evidence.

Standards Formed from the Record

The 2001 work access and tax recognition record, the 2007 DOT / FTA transit review, and the 2022 DMV vehicle operation recognition record help define how a reviewer should analyze skates or another non-standard mobility aid without reducing the decision to appearance.

Work

Work access standard

A non-standard mobility aid may be relevant when it supports a person's ability to reach, perform, or sustain work activity.

Transit

Transit access standard

A non-standard mobility aid should be reviewed by disability related function, physical accommodation, actual safety risk, and effective alternatives. A device should not be rejected merely because it is commonly associated with recreation.

Biomechanics

Biomechanics support standard

When available, biomechanics evidence can help explain why a non-standard mobility aid supports functional movement, reduces ballistic loading, or changes the movement pattern that creates the impairment.

Vehicle operation

Vehicle operation standard

Direct threat review should examine actual control, setting, restrictions, licensing conditions, and demonstrated safe use, rather than assuming that a non-standard mobility aid is unsafe by appearance alone.

Transportation

Transportation access standard

An alternative is not effective simply because it moves a person from one address to another. Review must consider access burden, route duration, vehicle mode, transfer requirements, reliability, and whether the alternative preserves functional access.

Evidence boundary

Evidence boundary standard

Medical notes, biomechanics records, wearable data, route records, vehicle records, videos, and public documents may support review when source, context, quality limits, and interpretation boundaries are preserved.

Source Linked Review Path

The source trail should remain separate from interpretation. Reviewers can use the federal transit timeline, mobility-aid standards, transportation standards, direct threat analysis, and references page to understand how the standards apply without treating any record as a universal access rule.
