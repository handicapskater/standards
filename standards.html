Review the function, not the stereotype

Non-standard mobility aid review should be individualized, source linked, and environment specific.

Evidence Standard

A standards record should preserve separate findings for functional need, physical accommodation, actual safety risk, effective alternatives, and evidence boundaries. Source linked evidence, including sensor data where available, may support review, but raw metrics should not replace individualized assessment.

Standards origin

Standards Developed from the HandicapSkater Record

These standards were developed from the HandicapSkater record, including documented review of skates as a non-standard mobility aid in work access, federal transit, vehicle operation, transportation access, and public accommodation settings.

The standards do not ask reviewers to accept a device by label alone. They ask reviewers to examine function, medical or disability related use, biomechanics where available, physical accommodation, actual safety risk, effective alternatives, and source linked evidence boundaries.

Review precedent standards

1. Function

Identify the mobility limitation and the functional role of the device. Appearance alone should not decide whether something is a mobility aid.

2. Physical accommodation

Review fit, clearance, stopping, transfer, crowding, storage, and the specific built environment.

3. Direct threat

Actual risk matters. Speculation, discomfort, or recreation assumptions should not substitute for evidence.

4. Narrow restrictions

Where risk exists in one location, consider whether access remains possible in concourses, sidewalks, waiting areas, or other safer zones.

5. Effective alternatives

Alternatives should be assessed for whether they are equally effective or impose avoidable access burden.

6. Reviewable record

Document the facts, sources, risks, mitigations, and reasons for the decision.

Transportation alternatives require their own review because vehicle mode, seat position, route duration, and shared routing can determine whether access is effective or burdensome.

Evidence boundaries

Metric Evidence Boundaries

Metric evidence can support review of a non-standard mobility aid only when the record preserves what was measured, where it was measured, how it was measured, source authority, quality limits, and interpretation boundaries.

Metrics should support individualized review. They should not replace function, physical accommodation, actual direct threat, medical or disability related use, biomechanics review, or effective alternative analysis.

  • Metrics can describe recorded signals and context.
  • Metrics cannot decide acceptance by themselves.
  • Review must preserve source authority and missing data limits.
  • Sensor evidence should be interpreted with setting, activity, and accommodation context.
  • The individual HandicapSkater corpus and dashboard artifacts live on HandicapSkater.com, while HandicapSkater.org defines general standards.
