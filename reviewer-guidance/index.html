REVIEWER DECISION FRAMEWORK

Reviewer Guidance

Separate observed facts, limitations, individual context, generalized standards, actual risk, and alternatives.

Use the checklistIndividual Case Study & Evidence

Review and record

Use the full ten-step framework, then make the decision traceable.

  1. Define the function and comparator.Record the claimed mobility task and relevant activity, aid, or trip.
  2. Review output and burden.Keep supplied units, sample counts, dates, and body-coupling context visible.
  3. Check repeated observations.Preserve mixed direction, missingness, and unequal conditions.
  4. Assess evidence quality.Record source authority, provenance, validation, limitations, and reproducibility.
  5. Separate observation from diagnosis.Wearable estimates do not directly measure pain or independently establish a clinical conclusion.
  6. Assess actual individualized risk.Use setting-specific evidence rather than unfamiliarity or appearance.
  7. Test effective alternatives.Determine whether each proposed alternative preserves meaningful access.
  8. State uncertainty.Identify what is unknown and what new information could change the decision.
  9. Explain the decision.Tie each restriction or accommodation to the documented record.
  10. Avoid universalizing the example.Individual case evidence illustrates a method; it does not establish a universal rule.

Before review

Gather the functional claim, actual environment, comparison, source material, and requested accommodation.

During review

Invite correction, distinguish fact from inference, and consider less-restrictive options.

After review

Record reasons, evidence limits, alternatives considered, conditions, and a path for reassessment.

Use this checklist to make a decision traceable; the outcome remains tied to the individual facts, setting, and governing authority.
