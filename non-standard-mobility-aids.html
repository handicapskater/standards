Mobility aids outside default categories

Non-standard mobility aids

A device can support disability access even when it does not look like a conventional cane, walker, wheelchair, or scooter.

Concept

The non-standard mobility aid concept asks whether a person uses the device because of a mobility disability, what function it serves, whether it can be physically accommodated, and whether it creates an actual direct threat to safety in the specific environment.

Skates

The 2007 FTA record used roller skates terminology. This site uses inline skates or skates for the current mobility aid framing. The 2007 BART record should be described as analyzing skates as a mobility aid in an environment-specific transit setting. It should not be described as resolving every setting or every safety question.

Powered devices

DOJ's OPDMD rules use powered device language for other power-driven mobility devices, including examples such as Segways and golf carts. OPDMD is not identical to skates, but it reflects the broader principle that people with disabilities may use devices that do not fit traditional categories.

Examples of Non-Standard Mobility Aid Questions

Inline skates are one worked example. The same questions can apply to powered devices, adaptive cycles, rolling devices, wearable supports, custom mobility configurations, and other non-standard mobility aids.

Movement pathway

Does the device change how the person moves?

Functional burden

Does it reduce pain, fatigue, instability, shock, or functional burden?

Setting safety

Can it be operated safely in this setting?

Mitigation

Can risk be mitigated by route, speed, assistance, timing, or conditions?

Alternative access

Does an alternative actually preserve effective access?

Source support

What source-linked evidence supports the request?

Function Comes Before Familiarity

A mobility aid should be reviewed by the function it serves, the setting where it is used, the user's control, and the available mitigations. Familiar appearance is not the standard.

Skates are one worked example of a non-standard mobility aid. The standards question is whether the device serves a disability related mobility function, can be physically accommodated in the setting, and creates an actual direct threat to safety.

Review principles

Do not assume recreation

Appearance should not substitute for functional review.

Separate powered from non-powered

OPDMD is powered-device doctrine; skates are better discussed as a separate non-standard mobility aid example analyzed by FTA.

Use actual risk

Public entities and transportation providers should consider physical accommodation and direct safety threat, not categorical assumptions.
