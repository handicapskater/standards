Non-Standard Mobility Aid Standards

Policy and evidence frameworks for mobility aids that do not fit default categories.

HandicapSkater.org is a public standards and policy site for non-standard mobility aids, individualized accommodation review, and source-linked accessibility review guidance.

What this site does

HandicapSkater.org separates standards from case evidence. It provides public review frameworks, source history and review context, and doctrine summaries for non-standard mobility aids. The individual evidence record lives on HandicapSkater.com.

This site helps agencies, transit providers, businesses, courts, public entities, and accessibility reviewers evaluate non-standard mobility aids without relying on appearance, stereotypes, or recreation assumptions. The goal is not categorical approval or categorical exclusion. The goal is documented, individualized review of function, physical accommodation, actual safety risk, effective alternatives, and avoidable access burden.

Evidence standards are provided separately for source linked records and review boundaries.

Review framework

A standards review should identify the device’s function, the setting, the actual safety facts, the available alternatives, and the record needed for review.

Function

What limitation does the device mitigate, and how does it support access or safe movement?

Physical accommodation

Can the device fit, maneuver, stop, wait, board, or be stored in the specific setting?

Direct threat

Is the asserted risk based on actual facts, or on speculation or unfamiliarity with non-standard mobility aids?

Effective alternatives

Do proposed alternatives provide comparable access, or do they impose avoidable access burden?

Reviewability

Can the decision be checked, corrected, updated, and tied to source-linked facts?

Standards for individualized review

This site provides review frameworks for deciding whether a mobility aid can be physically accommodated, whether a restriction is tied to the actual environment, and whether a safety concern is supported by facts.
