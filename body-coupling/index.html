PRINCIPLE 03 · BODY–ENVIRONMENT INTERFACE

Body Coupling

A mobility or transportation mode cannot be evaluated from its label alone. Review how the body is supported, controlled, positioned, moved, and exposed in the actual configuration.

Review the dimensionsTransportation framework

Describe the interface before comparing modes

Active control, rolling support, single-leg support, fixed seating, restraints, transfers, vibration, turns, stops, route duration, and waiting can couple the same body to movement in different ways.

Control

Active operator, active rider, assisted movement, or passive passenger exposure.

Support

Foot contact, wheels, seat, back support, restraints, handholds, and mobility-aid continuity.

Exposure

Posture, duration, transfers, acceleration, braking, vibration, route, and recovery context.

Keep mechanics and physiology separate

Mechanical motion exposure and physiologic or autonomic response answer different questions. Neither should be collapsed into one universal mode ranking, and wearables do not directly measure pain.
