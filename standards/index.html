Engineering Principles

Describe the body–environment system, identify actual risk, and evaluate function before appearance.

01 · Body–environment interface

How is the body coupled to the task?

The same person can experience active rolling, walking, fixed seating, restraints, transfers, vibration, and waiting as mechanically different systems.

ControlActive operator, active rider, assisted movement, or passive passenger.
SupportFoot contact, wheels, seat, back support, restraints, and handholds.
ExposurePosture, duration, transfers, acceleration, braking, vibration, and route.

Body coupling is the physical and control relationship among a person, a mobility or transportation device, and the surrounding environment.

Mechanical domain
Contact, support, posture, acceleration, movement, vibration, and impact exposure.
Physiologic domain
Heart-rate or autonomic context may be reviewed separately; it is not a substitute for mechanics or pain.

FSI and CSS are project-defined, versioned derived metrics with distinct formula identities, component coverage, and sample scope.

02 · Evidence before assumption

What actual risk is supported?

A testable risk statement makes review more consistent, exposes missing information, and creates room for targeted mitigation.

ObservedRecord current, setting-specific facts and the authority for each fact.
UncertainKeep missing or conflicting information visible instead of filling it with inference.
ReducibleTest conditions, assistance, route, timing, positioning, and alternatives.

An actual-risk statement identifies a plausible harm and connects it to supported facts about the person, task, device, environment, duration, and control strategy.

Risk statement
Specific harm + supported mechanism + setting + likelihood + severity.
Evidence record
Retain the wearable measurement scope, task, environment, duration, and control strategy.

03 · Function before appearance

What function does the aid provide?

A device’s familiar category does not establish effectiveness, and unfamiliar form does not establish actual risk.

FunctionWhat disability-related task does the aid enable or make more sustainable?
Fit and controlHow is the aid used, controlled, supported, transported, and managed?
Evidence and alternativesWhat facts support the request, and would another option provide equivalent function?

A non-standard mobility aid is a generalized review category for an aid whose disability-related function may not be obvious from its conventional label or appearance.

Use the term
When the review question concerns unfamiliar form and disability-related function.
Individualized review
Assess function, task, environment, control strategy, and actual risk from the specific record.

“Non-traditional mobility aid” may be appropriate when describing the individual adaptation; preserve the source’s original terminology when quoting it.

04 · Apply the system

Ten-step review framework

Document what evidence is present, missing, disputed, or limited at every step.

  1. Define the claimed mobility function.State the task the person is trying to complete.
  2. Define the comparison activity.Identify walking, rolling, transport, another aid, or another relevant comparator.
  3. Measure functional output.Document completion, useful distance, duration, continuity, and access outcome.
  4. Measure physiological and movement burden.Keep source, unit, scope, sample count, and limitations visible.
  5. Describe body coupling.Record posture, support, control, contact, transfers, and environment.
  6. Review repeated N-of-1 observations.Preserve mixed results, missingness, and unequal conditions.
  7. Assess source authority and reproducibility.Check provenance, validation, versions, and data-through dates.
  8. Separate observations and interpretations.Retain each wearable signal’s source and measurement scope.
  9. Assess actual risk.Use setting-specific evidence rather than unfamiliarity or appearance.
  10. Consider effective less-restrictive alternatives.Test whether another option preserves meaningful access.
