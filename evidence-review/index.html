OPTIONAL SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

Use proportionate evidence.

Evidence can support a functional review, but advanced instrumentation is not a prerequisite for an unfamiliar mobility aid to be evaluated.

Evidence hierarchy

Start with the requested function.

Use the least intrusive evidence adequate for the setting and decision. Preserve source, date, scope, limitations, and uncertainty.

  1. Individual functional statementDescribe the requested use and disability-related mobility function.
  2. Direct observationUse a setting-appropriate demonstration where relevant.
  3. Professional functional documentationUse only what is needed for the review.
  4. Prior scoped historyConsider prior accommodations, decisions, and successful use.
  5. Technical evidenceUse device, biomechanical, technical, or rehabilitation evidence where relevant.
  6. Optional objective measurementActivity history, route data, wearables, or other instrumentation may support review.

Scope matters

Measurement is not a pain meter.

Objective evidence can describe movement, exposure, physiological context, or mobility output. It should not be relabeled as direct proof of pain, diagnosis, medical necessity, legal entitlement, or universal function.

WHOOP, Polar, Kubios, Strava, FSI, and CSS are not prerequisites. When used, they remain optional, source-specific evidence with their own scope and limitations.

Evidence quality

Make limitations inspectable.

Record source, scope, date range, transformation, missingness, version, and what the evidence does not establish. Do not silently combine activity, recovery, session, or route data into a new claim.
