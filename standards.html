General review method

Core Review Standards

These standards were developed from the HandicapSkater record but are written for general non-standard mobility aid review. They do not guarantee acceptance of any device in every setting.

Function before appearance

A device should not be rejected merely because it is unfamiliar, recreational in ordinary use, or not listed in a traditional mobility-aid category.

Reviewer question: What function does the device perform for this person in this setting?

Disability-related use

Review should ask whether the device is used because of disability-related movement limitation, pain, fatigue, instability, shock sensitivity, or functional burden.

Reviewer question: Is the device connected to disability-related function, not mere convenience?

Actual risk, not stereotypes

Safety restrictions should be based on actual risk in the specific setting, not generalized assumptions about the device.

Reviewer question: What is the specific risk, in this location, under these conditions, and can it be mitigated?

Effective alternative access

An alternative is not effective merely because it exists. It must preserve meaningful access without imposing avoidable functional burden.

Reviewer question: Does the alternative actually provide comparable access for this person?

Movement mode matters

Review should consider whether different movement modes impose different physical burdens.

Reviewer question: Does the device change the movement pathway in a way that matters to disability access?

Source-linked evidence review

Evidence should be traceable to its source and separated by type: agency letters, court access records, videos, route records, wearable data, medical notes, pleadings, disputed claims, and administrative determinations.

Wearable metrics may support review but do not decide legal entitlement by themselves.

Individualized direct-threat review

A direct-threat or safety denial must consider individual ability, setting, actual risk, mitigation, and evidence.

Review direct-threat analysis

Transportation burden review

Transportation accommodation must consider not only whether a person is transported, but whether the transportation mode imposes avoidable burden.

Review transportation accommodation

Device-neutral standards

The standard should not be “skates are always allowed” or “skates are always forbidden.” The standard should be a review method for any non-standard mobility aid.

Standards origin

Standards Developed from the HandicapSkater Record

These standards were developed from the HandicapSkater record, including documented review of skates as a non-standard mobility aid in work access, federal transit, vehicle operation, transportation access, and public accommodation settings.

The standards do not ask reviewers to accept a device by label alone. They ask reviewers to examine function, medical or disability related use, biomechanics where available, physical accommodation, actual safety risk, effective alternatives, and source linked evidence boundaries.

Review precedent standards

1. Function

Identify the mobility limitation and the functional role of the device. Appearance alone should not decide whether something is a mobility aid.

2. Physical accommodation

Review fit, clearance, stopping, transfer, crowding, storage, and the specific built environment.

3. Direct threat

Actual risk matters. Speculation, discomfort, or recreation assumptions should not substitute for evidence.

4. Narrow restrictions

Where risk exists in one location, consider whether access remains possible in concourses, sidewalks, waiting areas, or other safer zones.

5. Effective alternatives

Alternatives should be assessed for whether they are equally effective or impose avoidable access burden.

6. Reviewable record

Document the facts, sources, risks, mitigations, and reasons for the decision.

Transportation alternatives require their own review because vehicle mode, seat position, route duration, and shared routing can determine whether access is effective or burdensome.

Evidence boundaries

Metric Evidence Boundaries

Metric evidence can support review of a non-standard mobility aid only when the record preserves what was measured, where it was measured, how it was measured, source authority, quality limits, and interpretation boundaries.

Metrics should support individualized review. They should not replace function, physical accommodation, actual direct threat, medical or disability related use, biomechanics review, or effective alternative analysis.

  • Metrics can describe recorded signals and context.
  • Metrics cannot decide acceptance by themselves.
  • Review must preserve source authority and missing data limits.
  • Sensor evidence should be interpreted with setting, activity, and accommodation context.
  • The individual HandicapSkater corpus and dashboard artifacts live on HandicapSkater.com, while HandicapSkater.org defines general standards.
