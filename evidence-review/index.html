ONE REVIEW METHOD

Methods

Connect function, observed burden, source quality, transportation context, actual risk, alternatives, and a traceable decision.

Evidence review

Review output and burden together

Define the task and comparator first. Review functional output, physiological and movement burden, body coupling, repeated N-of-1 observations, source authority, and source quality.

  1. State the mobility function and comparison.
  2. Keep observed values in visible supplied units.
  3. Show sample counts and data-through dates.
  4. Preserve missing values and mixed results.
  5. Identify source scope and authority.
  6. State limitations beside interpretations.
  7. Check whether the result is reproducible and versioned.

What a mobility reviewer should notice

Route evidence documents repeated real-world mobility, distance, duration, continuity, and environment.

Repeated function

Ask whether routes show a sustained mobility pattern rather than an isolated recreational event.

Distance and duration

Treat distance as functional output and duration as exposure context; neither is automatically burden.

Source connection

Relate route records to compatible wearable and activity evidence while preserving source identity.

Transportation context

Consider how the person reached and left the activity, including transfers, waiting, and mobility-aid handling.

Environmental context

Weather and geography provide route context.

Coverage

Record route completion, source identity, distance, duration, environment, and missing fields.

Evidence quality

Make confidence inspectable

Quality is communicated through source scope, versions, counts, explicit missingness, limitations, and reproducible lineage—not certainty language.

Source and scope

Identify the device, data type, date range, cohort authority, session or recovery scope, and transformation lineage.

Completeness

Show samples, duplicates, rejection reasons, missingness, quality flags, and what cannot be compared.

Reproducibility

Preserve versions, immutable source hashes where public-safe, deterministic output, and distinct scientific identities.

Terminology and source boundaries

FSI means Fractal Stability Index. CSS means Cohort Similarity Score. Tensor v0.3 is a separately versioned scientific identity. Descriptive return direction is not CSS, and a legacy burden ratio is not FSI.

WHOOP overnight recovery HRV/RMSSD is longitudinal recovery context. Kubios / Polar H10 session HRV is episodic session evidence. Neither scope should be silently substituted for the other.

Wearable signals retain device, session, cohort, metric, and source-specific measurement scope.

Longitudinal evidence standards

Review pairing, references, grain, and authority explicitly

Longitudinal activity summaries and episodic sensor windows can support one evidence system while retaining distinct scientific questions and authority.

Paired mobility context

When two transport observations bracket the same mobility event, preserve the outing identity. Pairing the observations can control outing-level context better than treating the two legs as independent populations.

Reference-distribution governance

An “extreme” physiological observation depends on the comparison population. Require a declared historical window, cohort composition, threshold, minimum reference support, and sensitivity analysis.

Measurement grain

Activity-level wearable summaries and fixed 30-second physiology-mechanics windows answer different questions. Do not silently pool activity, session, recovery, and window grains.

Multiplicity

When multiple mechanical features, thresholds, responses, or lags are tested, require a declared inference family and multiplicity policy. Keep raw and adjusted results visible together.

Effective-event authority

Reconciled cross-source identities govern current scientific views. Historical versions remain queryable for audit and must not leak into current-event counts.

Cohort versus route

Scientific cohort membership and route or location context are separate governed fields. Do not define a functional cohort by one geography or silently infer route membership.

Dependency-aware correction

A post-publication authority correction requires an input-manifest diff. If outcome-bearing populations are unchanged, preserve immutable inference and refresh only dependent descriptive projections.

Two readiness dimensions

Report scientific-authority completeness separately from archive-operational housekeeping. Preserve operational warnings without presenting them as scientific defects.

Result roles

Label each output as evaluated, sensitivity, screening, or future experiment. A discovered breakpoint is screening evidence, and a future contract cannot retroactively promote a sensitivity result.

Future experiment contract

A prospective mechanical-response experiment should predeclare the primary mechanical feature and threshold, physiological response, lag, inference family, multiplicity policy, ride/date clustering, and sample-sufficiency rule before new data or result inspection.

Transportation accommodation

Review the actual configuration

Review the actual trip, body coupling, vehicle environment, duration, routing, transfer demands, and effectiveness of alternatives. A sedan, van, bus, rail trip, taxi, or other mode may differ by posture, seat position, vibration, shock, route duration, transfers, shared routing, waiting, and the person’s coupling to the vehicle. Do not reduce the decision to vehicle labels.

Useful access

Does the trip reach the destination in a functionally usable way?

Avoidable access burden

Which waiting, routing, transfer, posture, vehicle, or mobility-aid handling demands add burden that a feasible accommodation could reduce?

Effective alternatives

Is a proposed substitute actually effective and less restrictive for the individual?

No transport category is inherently equivalent across people and trips; review remains individualized, setting-specific, and evidence-led.

Reviewer decision record

Make the reasoning traceable

Use the engineering framework, then record what was known, uncertain, considered, and decided.

Before review

Gather the functional claim, actual environment, comparison, source material, and requested accommodation.

During review

Invite correction, distinguish fact from inference, and consider less-restrictive options.

After review

Record reasons, evidence limits, alternatives considered, conditions, and a path for reassessment.

  1. Define the function and comparator.
  2. Review output, burden, body coupling, repeated observations, and source quality.
  3. Separate observations and interpretations.
  4. Assess actual individualized risk and effective alternatives.
  5. State uncertainty and explain the decision.
