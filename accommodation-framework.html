Review framework

Individualized accommodation review

A practical checklist for agencies, transit providers, businesses, courts, public entities, and accessibility reviewers.

An accommodation review should distinguish discomfort with non-standard mobility aids from evidence of actual direct threat.

Five-Step Accommodation Review

This framework helps reviewers evaluate non-standard mobility aids without relying on appearance, stereotypes, or device labels alone.

1. Identify the functional limitation.

What movement, endurance, posture, shock, fatigue, or access barrier is being addressed?

2. Identify the device or requested modification.

What device, route, vehicle configuration, timing, assistance, or policy modification is requested?

3. Evaluate physical accommodation in the setting.

Can the device or modification fit the route, facility, vehicle, program, or service?

4. Evaluate actual risk and possible mitigation.

What specific risk is alleged, what evidence supports it, and can narrower conditions mitigate it?

5. Evaluate whether alternatives are meaningfully effective.

Does the alternative preserve functional access, or does it impose avoidable mobility burden?

The framework does not guarantee acceptance of every device in every setting. It supports individualized review based on function, setting, actual risk, and effective access.

Accommodation Review Sequence

Use this sequence: identify the disability related mobility use, identify the device function, review physical accommodation, evaluate actual direct threat, consider narrower restrictions, compare effective alternatives, and document the individualized conclusion.

For transportation-specific review, use the Transportation Accommodation page to evaluate vehicle mode, seat position, shared routing, route duration, posture, reliability, feasible rail connections, and lower burden alternatives.
