PRINCIPLE 01 · INDIVIDUALIZED REVIEW

Direct Threat

Evaluate a specific person, aid, task, and environment using current objective information—not appearance or category alone.

Purpose

What decision does this principle improve?

It turns a general safety concern into a documented, setting-specific review that can identify both actual risk and effective risk-reduction measures.

SpecifyName the asserted harm, affected person, activity, location, timing, and operating conditions.
SupportDistinguish current objective information from unfamiliarity, labels, and speculation.
ReduceConsider reasonable measures and effective less-restrictive alternatives before exclusion.

Definition

What counts as a direct-threat analysis?

A direct-threat analysis is an individualized assessment of a significant risk of substantial harm under the actual circumstances. The reasoning should identify the evidence used, uncertainty that remains, likely duration and severity, probability, and measures that could mitigate the risk.

Not enough
“Unusual,” “recreational,” “against policy,” or “looks unsafe.”
Required
A documented connection between facts, the setting, the asserted harm, and the proposed response.

Application

What should the written record contain?

  1. Claimed mobility function and requested accommodation.
  2. Actual environment, route, duration, traffic, and operational constraints.
  3. Objective evidence supporting likelihood and severity.
  4. Information that is missing, disputed, or time-limited.
  5. Risk-reduction measures and less-restrictive alternatives considered.
  6. Reasons connecting the evidence to the final decision.
